How to Read a Currency Strength Meter

📅 Page reviewed: 11 August 2026 · Reading the Market

A currency strength meter answers a deceptively simple question: of the eight major currencies, which are strong right now and which are weak? It sounds basic, but used well it changes how you select pairs — and pair selection is half of any FX decision.

The core idea: trade strength against weakness

Every currency pair is a relative bet. If your view is "the dollar looks strong," the meter helps you find the weakest counterpart to express it against — a strong-USD view lands very differently in USD/JPY (if the yen is also firm) than in USD/ZAR-style weak links. The widest strength gap between two currencies usually identifies the pair where a trend has the least internal resistance.

Reading gaps, not levels

A single currency's score matters less than the spread between two. A 65 next to a 60 is a coin-flip pair; a 65 against a 35 is a pair with a clear story. When scanning a strength board, look for the extremes first — strongest vs weakest — then check whether that pair's spread and liquidity make it practical to follow.

Strength boards and news sentiment together

Our board scores strength from the tone of each currency's news flow, so a wide gap comes with an explanation attached: the drivers behind each side's score. That answers the natural follow-up question — why is this currency strong? — without leaving the page. A gap backed by a fresh, developing story reads differently from one built on week-old news.

Common mistakes

Three habits undermine most strength-meter use: treating scores as trade signals rather than a selection filter; ignoring the time axis (a currency that's been at the top for a week is a different proposition from one that arrived there this morning); and forgetting event risk — the widest gap in the world matters little an hour before a rate decision on either leg. The economic calendar belongs next to any strength read.

Frequently asked questions

What does a currency strength meter show?

A relative ranking of the major currencies — which sides of the market are currently strong or weak — so you can choose pairs that pit strength against weakness.

Should I trade directly off a strength meter?

It works better as a pair-selection filter than a signal. The meter tells you where the widest gaps are; timing and risk management still come from your own process.

Why do strength readings change during the day?

New data releases, central bank comments and shifting risk tone all update each currency's news flow — and the readings with it.

Which pair should I watch when USD is strongest?

Pair it against the weakest major on the board at the time. The widest gap typically offers the clearest expression of the strength story.
