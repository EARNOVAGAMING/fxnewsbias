What Moves AUD/USD?

📅 Page reviewed: 11 August 2026 · Pair Guides

AUD/USD is the G10's cleanest expression of global growth optimism. Australia's export mix, iron ore, coal, LNG, ties its currency to Chinese demand and world trade, and its deep, liquid market makes the Aussie the instrument of choice for expressing a broad risk view. When markets feel good about global growth, AUD/USD usually knows first.

1. China, above everything

China buys more of Australia's exports than any other country by a wide margin. Chinese PMIs, property-sector news, stimulus announcements and trade data all flow through to the Aussie, often more powerfully than Australian domestic data. If AUD/USD is moving and Australia's calendar is empty, look at China first.

2. Commodity prices

Iron ore is the headline link, it's Australia's largest export, but the broader industrial-commodity complex matters too. Sustained commodity rallies improve Australia's terms of trade and tend to support the currency; sharp commodity selloffs weigh on it. The link is loose day-to-day but strong over weeks.

3. Risk appetite

The Aussie is a classic risk-on currency: it tends to rise when equities rally and volatility falls, and it tends to fall, often alongside NZD and against JPY and CHF, when markets de-risk. This is why AUD/JPY specifically is watched as a risk barometer: it pairs the risk-on currency with the classic safe haven. Our risk-on / risk-off guide covers that mechanic in depth.

4. The RBA and the rates gap

The Reserve Bank of Australia matters most when its path diverges from the Fed's. Australian CPI (quarterly, unlike most peers, a scheduling quirk that concentrates its impact) and employment data drive RBA expectations. A hawkish RBA against a softening Fed is historically one of the friendliest backdrops the pair gets.

Watching the flow

Aussie-moving news splits across three time zones: Australian data overnight, Chinese data in the Asian morning, and the global risk tone through London and New York. A sentiment read across the whole day's coverage helps stitch those threads together, our dashboard tracks how the AUD news flow is leaning against the USD side in close to real time.

Frequently asked questions

Why does Chinese data move AUD/USD?

China is Australia's largest export customer by a wide margin, so Chinese demand signals feed directly into Australia's trade outlook and its currency.

Is AUD/USD a risk-on currency pair?

Yes, the Aussie typically strengthens when global risk appetite improves and weakens in de-risking episodes, which is why it's widely used as a risk proxy.

Why is Australian CPI only quarterly?

Australia's statistics bureau publishes full CPI quarterly (with a partial monthly indicator), which concentrates rate-expectation repricing into fewer, larger events.

What is the relationship between AUD/USD and AUD/JPY?

AUD/JPY pairs the risk-on Aussie against the safe-haven yen, making it an even purer risk barometer; the two pairs usually move together in risk episodes.
