What Moves GBP/USD?

📅 Page reviewed: 11 August 2026 · Pair Guides

GBP/USD, "cable", trades like a sharper-edged cousin of EUR/USD. The same dollar-side forces apply, but the pound adds its own volatility: UK inflation has run persistently hotter than peers in recent cycles, and sterling carries a political risk premium that can appear with little warning.

1. The Bank of England–Fed gap

As with every dollar pair, the rate-expectation gap does most of the work. The UK twist is that the Bank of England has often faced stickier inflation than the Fed or ECB, forcing it to stay restrictive longer than markets expected. Repricing of BoE expectations, often triggered by a single CPI or wage print, moves cable hard.

2. UK inflation and wage data

UK CPI (especially services inflation) and average-earnings releases are cable's most reliable movers after the US majors. Services inflation has been the BoE's stated focus, so a surprise there tends to shift rate expectations directly. UK data lands at 06:00 UTC (07:00 London), meaning cable often sets its daily tone before most of Europe is at their desks.

3. Political risk premium

Sterling prices political uncertainty faster than most G10 currencies, a legacy visible in episodes from the Brexit referendum to the 2022 gilt crisis. Leadership challenges, fiscal announcements and budget events can dominate cable for days regardless of what the data says. When UK politics heats up, the news flow matters more than the calendar.

4. Volatility profile

Cable's average daily range runs meaningfully wider than EUR/USD's. That cuts both ways: moves are larger when you're right, and stops need more room. The pair is most active through the London morning and the London–New York overlap.

Reading the tape

Because sterling stories break fast, a wage print at 06:00, a political headline mid-afternoon, a sentiment read on the day's GBP coverage is a useful complement to the calendar. Frequently asked questions

Why is GBP/USD called cable?

The name dates to the 1850s transatlantic telegraph cable that carried the London–New York exchange rate.

What UK data moves GBP/USD the most?

CPI, particularly services inflation, and average-earnings data, because both feed directly into Bank of England rate expectations.

Is GBP/USD more volatile than EUR/USD?

Historically yes; cable's typical daily range runs wider, and political headlines add episodic volatility the euro rarely matches.

What time of day is GBP/USD most active?

From the London open through the London–New York overlap. UK data at 06:00 UTC often sets the day's direction early.
