AUD Falls Below 0.6950 as June CPI Misses, RBA Hold Locked In

London is opening — here is the forex sentiment setup heading into the European session. Australian Dollar (AUD) faces the strongest bearish news pressure across the majors today. Here is what triggered the move and where it goes from here.

By FXNewsBias Team·Published Wed, 29 Jul 2026·4 min read

Australian Dollar, AUD, 28/100 — Bearish — slides sharply as softer-than-expected inflation data triggers heavy selling and threatens extended RBA pause.

Soft Australian CPI print cements currency weakness and opens the door to fresh downside targets as the forex market reprices rate expectations.

What Happened

Australia's June inflation cooled to 3.8% year-on-year, undershooting the 4.0% forecast and igniting a swift reversal in AUD sentiment. The softer-than-expected consumer price index immediately triggered heavy selling across major Australian Dollar pairs, with AUD/USD breaking decisively below the critical 0.6950 support level. This price action reflects trader conviction that weaker inflation data seals the case for an extended Reserve Bank of Australia hold, removing near-term rate hike expectations that had underpinned the Aussie's recent gains.

The breakdown carries real technical weight. With AUD/USD now vulnerable near 0.6950 following the CPI-driven collapse, analysts are eyeing 0.6750 as the next meaningful downside target. The speed and magnitude of the selloff underscore how pivotal inflation data has become for central bank policy expectations in the current cycle. Softer inflation naturally pushes back any near-term tightening, a narrative that weighs heavily on commodity-linked currencies like the Australian Dollar, which rely on growth and rate differentials for support.

Market Reaction

The broader forex market reacted by repricing Federal Reserve policy bets in real time, creating sharp divergence across major currency pairs. While the Australian Dollar crumbled, the Canadian Dollar benefited from relative stability, with USD/CAD unable to extend below its 200-hour moving average—a setup that underscores the AUD/CAD pair's widest sentiment gap in the session. The Canadian currency found support as Fed uncertainty tempered US Dollar bulls, even though the greenback itself scored 62/100 on renewed September rate-hike bets tied to sticky inflation expectations.

Risk sentiment tilted cautiously lower across commodities and emerging-market exchange rates, though the move remained contained ahead of the FOMC decision. The New Zealand Dollar and Euro both showed weakness, while sterling gained marginal ground as traders hedged USD downside risk. Gold consolidated near $4,000 and silver advanced despite Middle East tensions and broader Fed uncertainty, reflecting the complex interplay between safe-haven demand and rate expectations that currently dominates currency strength across the majors.

What's Driving the Move

Three key threads run through the bearish Australian Dollar story:

  1. Australia's June CPI inflation printed at 3.8% year-on-year versus a 4.0% forecast, triggering immediate selling and AUD/USD breakdown below 0.6950 support.
  2. Softer inflation data removes near-term RBA rate-hike expectations, shifting central bank policy bets toward an extended hold and reducing domestic rate support for the Australian currency.
  3. Broader Fed uncertainty and cautious USD positioning ahead of the FOMC decision dampened risk appetite and widened the currency strength gap between commodity-linked AUD and the relatively safer Canadian Dollar.

What to Watch Next

📈 Bull case for the move
A hawkish FOMC surprise—signaling September rate hikes or sticky core inflation persistence—could trigger a broad USD rally and risk-on rebound that pulls AUD/USD back above 0.6950 and stabilizes the Aussie. Equally, any evidence that Australian inflation expectations remain anchored and core CPI pressure abates could allow the RBA to signal a longer hold without triggering fresh capitulation in AUD positions.
📉 Risk to the view
If Fed speakers or the FOMC statement signal dovish tilt and softer near-term rate guidance, USD weakness would extend and AUD/USD could accelerate toward the 0.6750 target as rate differentials compress further. A weaker-than-expected US consumer confidence reading or explicit ECB easing bias would reinforce safe-haven flows and lock in AUD losses across major pairs.
