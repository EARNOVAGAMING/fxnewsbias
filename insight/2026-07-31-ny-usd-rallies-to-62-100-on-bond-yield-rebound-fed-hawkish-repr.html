USD Rallies to 62/100 on Bond Yield Rebound & Fed Hawkish Repricing

What Happened

The US Dollar extended gains on Friday as bond yields rebounded and investors repriced expectations for a less dovish Federal Reserve trajectory. According to ING analysis, post-Fed losses have begun to unwind as positioning normalizes, while the hawkish tone on inflation and economic resilience continues to underpin USD appeal. The Dollar Index is now seen returning to the 96.00–100.00 range, signaling a return to broader USD resilience after recent volatility.

Commodity markets reflected this renewed greenback strength: gold and silver both declined as the firmer dollar and hawkish Fed outlook weighed on precious metals demand. The combination of elevated US bond yields—a key pillar of dollar attractiveness—and central bank messaging has restored confidence in the currency's medium-term uptrend, even as some analysts flagged that the rally may be encountering serious headwinds.

Market Reaction

Across the broader forex market, the dollar's strength created a bifurcated backdrop: risk-sensitive currencies and commodity-linked names struggled, while traditional safe-haven flows supported the greenback. The Australian Dollar managed to hold firm despite weak Chinese data, as fading RBA rate hike bets reduced headwinds and dovish Fed repricing narrowed the rate differential pressure.

The widest sentiment divergence emerged between sterling and the euro. The British Pound sank to 48/100 Bearish as traders reassessed BoE interest rate hike expectations, while the euro—buoyed by unexpectedly higher core inflation at 2.5%—initially rebounded. However, EUR hit session lows against GBP, creating an unusual dynamic where the euro's hawkish inflation print failed to override sterling weakness. GBP/USD remains the key pair to monitor, with the pound's underperformance reflecting mounting doubt over UK rate tightening rather than broad-based dollar strength alone.

What's Driving the Move

Three key threads run through the bullish US Dollar story:

  1. Bond yields rebounded sharply on Friday, restoring the dollar's carry appeal and pushing DXY back toward the 96.00–100.00 range as outlined by BBH analysts.
  2. Post-Fed positioning unwinds have reversed recent losses, with investors repricing the Fed's inflation-fighting credibility and reducing bets for a steep rate-cut cycle.
  3. Gold and silver declined under pressure from the firmer dollar and hawkish Fed outlook, signaling that commodity traders have begun to price in a more persistent USD strength regime.

What to Watch Next

📈 Bull case for the move
A retest of the DXY 100.00 level would confirm that the Fed's hawkish tone has permanently reset market expectations for rate cuts, extending the dollar rally into next week. Any fresh US inflation print or central bank hawkish guidance during the Asia and London sessions could trigger fresh USD buying, especially if it reinforces the view that the Federal Reserve remains vigilant on price stability.
📉 Risk to the view
If risk sentiment suddenly improves—for example, on dovish central bank surprises or de-escalation in Middle East geopolitical tensions—the dollar could reverse sharply as investors rotate into higher-yielding, risk-correlated assets. A significant miss on US economic data in the coming week could also force a rapid repricing of Fed rate expectations and snap USD pairs back below recent support levels.

Watch for Asia's response to USD strength when the session opens at 00:13 UTC, particularly how Bank of Japan intervention dynamics and Chinese economic signals interact with the greenback's renewed momentum.
