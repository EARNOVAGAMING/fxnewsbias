What Happened

The Canadian Dollar retreated sharply on Friday as WTI crude slumped to near $80.50 on profit-taking, undermining the traditional oil-sensitive link that typically buoys the loonie. According to FXStreet, the Canadian Dollar weakened despite broader geopolitical risks in the Middle East, as USD recovery on fresh Fed rate hike expectations proved the dominant force. The weakness persisted even as the Strait of Hormuz saw increased traffic — a development that usually supports energy prices and, by extension, CAD.

Underlying the move is a forex market analysis showing that investor focus has shifted decisively toward US monetary policy expectations rather than commodity fundamentals. The retreat in crude prices combined with revived Fed bets created a dual headwind for the loonie, as both the energy backdrop dimmed and capital flows rotated into US dollar positions. This setup has left USD/CAD as the session's focal pair, with the greenback's relative strength overshadowing any near-term support that commodity producers might have expected.

Market Reaction

The broader FX session responded by widening the sentiment gap between the USD and CAD to 24 points — the day's largest divergence. USD/CAD itself extended higher, reflecting aggressive repricing of Federal Reserve rate expectations as gold weakness and Iran-linked geopolitical risks revived appetite for dollar safety. Beyond the loonie, the currency market showed mixed resilience: the euro slipped to near 1.1500 amid technical resistance, while the Australian Dollar and New Zealand Dollar held neutral ground after the Bank of Japan's policy hold.

The Bank of Japan's decision to leave rates at 1.00% with a hawkish 8-1 dissent vote created cascading effects across carry trades and risk sentiment, yet it ultimately benefited USD/JPY's rebound above 160 at the expense of lower-yielding pairs like CAD. This backdrop set the stage for a classic risk-repricing session in which commodity-linked and carry-sensitive currencies gave ground to the greenback's structural tailwinds.

What's Driving the Move

Three key threads run through the bearish Canadian Dollar story:

  1. WTI crude collapsed to near $80.50 on profit-taking, severing the traditional commodity support that underpins Canadian Dollar strength.
  2. Fed rate hike bets revived on the back of gold weakness and geopolitical risk appetite, pulling capital into USD positions and away from the loonie.
  3. The Bank of Japan's hawkish hold and rare Japan–South Korea intervention kept the yen pressured, redirecting safe-haven flows toward the US dollar rather than risk assets that CAD typically benefits from.

What to Watch Next

📈 Bull case for the move
A sharp rebound in crude prices above $82 on supply disruption fears, combined with a dovish pivot in Fed expectations from this week's PCE print, could stabilize USD/CAD and support CAD recovery. Any slip in US economic data early next week would reinforce that narrative and allow the loonie to reclaim its commodity-linked anchor.
📉 Risk to the view
If oil extends losses below $80 on weakening global demand signals or if the Fed's forward guidance on the next two meetings signals aggressive tightening, CAD could extend losses toward fresh session lows. A hawkish ECB decision could also amplify USD strength by contrast, creating additional headwinds for the exchange rate.

Watch for crude inventory data and any fresh central bank commentary over the Asia and early London sessions to gauge whether Friday's CAD weakness has further room to run.
